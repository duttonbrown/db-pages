Phase 1 — Inventory levels & low-stock alerts · LIVE

The keystone gap was simple: nothing in our system knew how much of a part we have. Without that number, no alert can fire. So Phase 1 created it.

What shipped

Done · live in Notion
  1. Two new fields on every part & supply: Qty on Hand and Reorder Point. The primitives everything else needs.
  2. Reorder points computed automatically from data we already had — annual usage and lead time — for 481 items (366 parts + 115 supplies). The formula: cover demand over the reorder lead time, plus a 50% safety buffer. Every number shows its own math, so it's checkable, not a black box.
  3. Low-stock detection that lists everything at or below its reorder point and formats the exact alert card — ready to wire to a nightly check.
✓ The moment someone enters on-hand counts, low-stock alerts light up. Next: a nightly check that posts to the Operations channel in Teams with an "Add to purchase queue" button.

Phase 2 — Real purchase orders + vendor master · IN PROGRESS

Turn the per-line request log into a real, vendor-grouped purchase order system — the one thing we'd otherwise still pay an MRP to do.

Vendor master — built & live

Done
A purchase order should default a vendor's terms, lead time, and contact — not make us re-type them every order. So the vendor directory grew up into a real vendor master.
  • Seven purchasing fields added to every vendor: Payment Terms, Lead Time (days), Min Order $, Account #, Contact Email, Default Incoterms, Currency.
  • Lead times converted from text to numbers across 37 vendors — so the system can compute order-by dates.
  • A Vendors interface in the Operations hub: search, filter by Import / Domestic, see every vendor's terms and what they supply, and start a PO straight from a vendor.
✓ The vendor master is the foundation every PO sits on. It's live now.

Purchase Orders database — designed & ready

One click away
A real PO is a header (who, when, totals, terms) plus its line items (what, how many, received). We modeled both against how MRPeasy and Katana structure a PO, then trimmed to exactly what a 10-person made-to-order shop needs — nothing more.

Lifecycle: Draft → Sent → Partially Received → Received → Closed → Cancelled. Receiving: a simple "quantity received" per line that handles partial shipments and rolls the PO status forward automatically — the overseas-split-shipment reality, without complexity.

The one thing it's waiting on: creating a database in Notion needs the page shared with our integration — a 30-second click. The schema is validated and the create-script is ready; the moment it's shared, the Purchase Orders and PO Line Items databases come to life and link to vendors, parts, and the request log.

How a purchase order will flow

StepWhat happens
1 · NeedA part runs low (alert) or someone requests it. It lands in the purchase queue.
2 · GroupOpen needs group by vendor into that vendor's open Draft PO — one accumulating draft per vendor.
3 · ReviewA person reviews the draft — quantities, prices, MOQ — and hits Send. Nothing reaches a vendor automatically.
4 · IssueA PO PDF is generated and emailed; the file is archived in SharePoint under 80_records / Purchase Orders with a clean PO-2026-0001 name.
5 · ReceiveAs boxes arrive, quantity received is logged per line; partials are handled; the PO rolls Draft → Sent → Partially Received → Received.

Phase 3 — Self-maintaining & predictive · later

Once Phase 2 is live, the last layer makes it run itself: on-hand counts that auto-deduct as orders ship (off the BOM engine we already run) and auto-add as POs are received; an all-parts shortage view; and reorder suggestions that tell us the stockout date, the quantity to order, and the date to order by. That's full MRP capability — two pieces of it better than the tools we'd have bought — at no monthly cost.

Where we are: Phase 1 (inventory alerts) is live. The vendor master and Vendors interface are live. The Purchase Orders database is designed, validated, and one click from existing. We've replaced most of what an MRP does — tuned to how Dutton Brown actually works — and we haven't spent a dollar a month to do it.
